# Client & Sponsor Acquisition (Digital + Physical)

## Overview

Emerge's growth is sponsor-first: local businesses pay a flat monthly fee
($200–500+/mo) for a **sponsored quest** — users finish a habit quest and get a
real reward (free smoothie, discount, class) redeemable at the sponsor's store.
The pitch is never "advertise with us". It's:

> *"Your paying customer completes a habit quest in Emerge and walks into YOUR
> store to redeem a real reward. You get a warm, motivated, already-interested
> customer — not an impression."*

The sponsor then markets the quest to their own customers, so the product
subsidizes its own distribution. This skill is the playbook to find, pitch,
close, and keep those clients — **businesses, community leaders, and creators**
— through both digital and physical/local tracks.

This skill is intended for the founder/agent acting as the one-person sales
engine. It shows the sequence and gives templates; the templates live in
`references/outreach-templates.md`.

## When to Use

- Building a lead list of sponsors/partners (businesses, clubs, creators).
- Writing a cold email / DM / WhatsApp pitch or follow-up sequence.
- Preparing for an in-person walk-in pitch (the 3-minute script).
- Structuring a creator/collaborator or community-leader deal.
- Pricing a sponsorship and calculating what ROI to promise.
- Drafting the one-page sponsor agreement.
- Nigeria-specific outreach norms (WhatsApp-first, naira pricing, campus
  communities).

**When NOT to use:** making content/ads/posts for an audience → that's
`digital-marketing`.

## The 60-Day Fast-Track to Sponsor #1 (and #2)

Follow this order; it is the pipeline.

1. **Days 1–10 — List & prep.** Build a 30-lead list: smoothie/café/gym within a
   three-block radius of the target campus + 5 campus clubs + 5 micro-creators.
   Set up a free CRM (HubSpot Free = 2 users, 1,000 contacts). Write the
   one-pager, sample quest, voucher mock, and the 3-touch follow-up sequence.
2. **Days 11–25 — Digital outbound.** 3 emails + 2 DMs per lead, in the cadence
   below. Track replies in the pipeline.
3. **Days 26–35 — Physical.** 2 walk-in outings/week per the 3-minute script,
   off-peak hours, one purchase-then-pitch (concierge) per store.
4. **Days 36–50 — Close Sponsor #1.** 2-week pilot quest at the launch-discounted
   fee → convert to monthly. Get #1 to refer you in one WhatsApp line to #2's
   owner.
5. **Days 51–60 — Sign #2 + first scorecard.** Publish the redemption
   scorecard; sign the bookstore/gym on the back of #1's result.

**Who to chase, in priority order:** (a) small local businesses (sponsors),
(b) community leaders (campus clubs, faith, professional associations),
(c) micro-creators (fitness/study/wellness).

## Track 1 — Digital Acquisition

### Cold outreach that converts (email + DM + WhatsApp)

Evidence (Woodpecker, woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/): B2B cold-email
reply rates average ~3.4%; **advanced personalization lifts replies to ~17–18%
vs ~7–9% basic**; small lists (<50) convert at ~5.8% vs 2.1% for 1,000+; the
real lever is **follow-ups** — 42% of replies come from them, one follow-up
+65.8% replies, yet 48% of reps never follow up.

- **Sequence: 4–7 touchpoints total.** First email → follow-up 1 at 3–4 days
  (highest performer, ~8.4%) → follow-up 2 at 5–7 days → follow-up 3 at 7–10
  days. After the 4th, spam risk triples — stop.
- **Every follow-up adds new value** (a stat, a case study, a new angle).
  "Just checking in" reduces meetings booked by 14%.
- **Format:** under ~80 words, one low-friction CTA ("Do you have 15 minutes
  this week to hear a 2-minute idea?"), subject lines 36–50 chars, avoid
  AI-sounding openers.
- **DM/WhatsApp** (native channels for local businesses): 2–3 sentences, one
  question, **no link in the first message** (link after they reply).
- Template (value-first, sponsor-customer angle) — full version in
  `references/outreach-templates.md`.

### LinkedIn, local SEO, and inbound

- LinkedIn: connect with the owner, reference something specific, keep the ask
  tiny; batch per common denominator (e.g., "gyms within 3 km of UNILAG").
- **Google Business Profile** is the free local-SEO base — claim it.
- **Social proof engine:** publish a weekly/monthly redemption stat
  ("Emerge users redeemed 140 rewards at partner cafés this month").
- Local landing pages per area ("sponsor a quest in Ibadan").

### Creators & community leaders

- **Micro over macro:** 43% of marketers prefer micro/nano; a nano influencer
  (~8k engaged) often beats a macro with 800k passive followers
  (influencermarketinghub.com/micro-influencer-marketing/,
  shopify.com/blog/influencer-pricing).
- **Fair structures (start cheap, pay on performance):** gifting + affiliate
  code first → flat micro-deal for 2–4 UGC posts → revenue share on redemptions
  driven by their code → a sponsored quest for their niche (both a creator deal
  AND the sponsor flywheel). Quote OLIPOP-style proof: creator codes took
  creator-driven sales from ~3% → ~20% of mix (IMH).
- **Community leaders:** the framing is **mutual value**, not sponsorship
  begging — members get a free useful tool + real rewards; the leader gets a
  "partner" badge, a challenge to promote, and activation. Campus clubs, faith
  youth groups, professional associations, alumni groups.
- Licenses: get usage rights for reused content (~$50 line) and audit for fake
  followers.

## Track 2 — Physical / Local Marketing

### The walk-in pitch (foot-traffic prospecting)

- **Go at off-peak** (mid-morning/weekday mid-afternoon) when the owner is
  present and unhurried; avoid lunch rush/weekends. Ask a quiet time if busy.
- **3-minute script** (full in references): open in 15s (not selling, 2-minute
  idea, now or pass) → value in 60s (sponsor quest = real reward = honest
  customers walk in) → proof + ask in 45s (a stat, the fee, a 5-min demo).
- **Concierge cross-promotion (differentiator):** buy a small item first, sit,
  then pitch — you become a customer who also offers value.
- Print a small in-store sign for each sponsor ("Reward: show this app quest at
  checkout") — signage is a proven local lever (~10% sales lift for stores that
  update signage; shopify.com/blog/local-marketing).

### Local partnerships & events

- **Adjacent, non-competing ecosystem:** the smoothie-bar + bookstore + gym
  triad in a college town. Sign #1 (smoothie) → "quit sugar/hydration" quest;
  #2 (bookstore) → "read 30 min/day" quest; #3 (gym) → "work out 2x/week"
  quest. Optionally bundle a "Campus Wellness Pack" where 3 businesses split a
  combined fee.
- **Events/market days:** staff a table (not a full pop-up), collect emails,
  live quest signups, sponsor QR. Flyers + QR → a landing page with UTM so you
  can measure which flyer converts.
- **Referral loops:** redeeming customers get a "bring a friend, both get a free
  shake" referral code; collect emails at checkout via digital receipts.

## Nigeria Playbook (the beachhead — critical)

- **WhatsApp is the connective tissue.** Pitch and follow up on WhatsApp (DM +
  voice note ≈ a meeting), not email. Sponsors already run WhatsApp broadcast
  lists of customers — the offer is "just forward this to your WhatsApp
  customers." Deliver voucher codes via WhatsApp Business.
- **Payments:** charge sponsors in naira via Paystack; offer **bank transfer /
  USSD** (Nigerians routinely pay by transfer, and USSD works on feature phones
  without banking apps) — never require a card.
- **Market context (Jan 2025, DataReportal):** 107M internet users (45.4%
  penetration), 38.7M social identities, Facebook 38.7M / TikTok 37.4M /
  YouTube 27M / Instagram 9.9M. ~80% of the workforce is informal.
- **Campus & community:** UNILAG alone enrolls ~57,000 students / ~8,500
  undergrads/year; campus gig economy moves ~$24M/month (TechCabal); youth =
  18–29 (Wikipedia). Pitch via market chairpersons, church youth leaders,
  campus "data vendors" (Airtel/MTN promo resellers) as redemption points and
  co-promoters.
- **Nigerian creators:** monetize via brand deals; some overprice — position
  Emerge as the **affordable, performance-based option** ("pay per redemption,
  not per post"). Micro-deal ranges are far below international anchors — test
  ₦50k–₦200k per campaign; treat every figure as a test, not fixed.

## Pricing, ROI, and the contract

- **Flat monthly (SaaS-style) sponsorship — recommended.** Tier by deliverable:
  Starter (1 quest/mo + reward + logo + monthly report) → Standard (1–2 quests,
  QR at redemption, banner, co-marketing story) → Partner (2–3 quests, signage
  kit, quarterly community challenge, full analytics). Per-campaign only as a
  2-week pilot to de-risk Sponsor #1, then convert to monthly.
- **ROI you can honestly promise** (only what the voucher platform can count —
  over-promising kills renewals): quest views/impressions (you control),
  voucher redemptions/foot traffic (unique codes scanned = walk-ins), repeat-visit
  rate, social/creator impressions (UTM-coded), and sponsor CAC (fee ÷ new
  customers) vs their paid-ad CAC. Deliver an end-of-month one-page scorecard —
  it is the renewal and upsell weapon.
- **One-page sponsor agreement:** parties → scope of sponsorship → fee &
  payment → term & renewal (30-day notice) → deliverables & obligations →
  usage/IP → termination → limitation of liability → signatures. Full template
  in `references/outreach-templates.md`.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Blasting** a big list — small, personalized lists convert 2–3x better.
2. **No follow-up** — 42% of replies come from follow-ups; 48% of reps never
   send one.
3. **Asking for likes/awareness** instead of a war-customer outcome — sell
   "warm foot traffic + repeat visits", never impressions.
4. **Over-promising ROI** — only promise what vouchers can count.
5. **Western-defaulting in Nigeria** — email instead of WhatsApp, card-only
   payments, ignoring campus/faith/market gatekeepers.
6. **Building before a signed sponsor** — sponsor-first (per strategy-page.md):
   no monetization code until sponsor #1 signs.
7. **Forgetting the scorecard** — without the monthly numbers, renewals and
   upsells are impossible.

## Red Flags — STOP and fix

- Pitching a business without a named reward they can redeem at their store.
- Sending an unpaste, generic email (no personal touch point).
- Tenure a "guaranteed customers" (guarantee the deliverable, promise targets).
- Pitching without a price on the table (state the flat fee up front).
- Approaching a store during rush hour.

## Speed checklist (before each pitch)

- [ ] Named the specific reward + how they redeem at the counter.
- [ ] Priced it (flat monthly fee) and named the currency.
- [ ] One personal touch point in the opener.
- [ ] One low-friction ask (10–15 min / 2-minute idea).
- [ ] Follow-ups queued with new value each touch.
- [ ] One-page agreement ready to send after "yes".
