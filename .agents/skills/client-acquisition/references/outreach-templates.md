# Outreach Templates, Scripts & Agreement

Copy-paste assets for the client-acquisition skill. Swap `[PLACEHOLDERS]` with
real data. Everything here is playbook guidance (a synthesis of 2025–26 research
cited in the SKILL.md); treat numbers you haven't verified locally as
`[VALIDATE]`.

## 1. The 3-minute walk-in pitch (physical)

Go at **off-peak** (mid-morning / weekday mid-afternoon); ask for the owner or
manager; request a quiet time if they're busy.

1. **Open (15s):**
   "Hi, I'm [Name], not selling anything big — I have a 2-minute idea that
   sends you customers. Do you have two minutes now, or should I pass at a
   quieter time?"
2. **Value (60s):**
   "There's a habit app, Emerge, popular with [students/gym people] here. We can
   make **[Your Store]** a *sponsored quest* — someone finishes a 2-week habit
   streak and they win a real reward from you: a free smoothie, a token, one
   class. Only honest-to-goodness customers come in to redeem."
3. **Proof + ask (45s):**
   "We did [X] redemptions last month at [other/our partner]. It's [NGN/₦
   equivalent of the fee] per month, cancel anytime after the first quest. Can I
   set up a 5-minute demo this week?"
4. **If no:**
   "No problem — can I leave this one-page sheet + QR? Share my number and I'll
   send you a sample quest tonight."

**Concierge variant:** buy a small item first, sit, then pitch — you're a
customer who also offers value. Do this at 3–5 nearby businesses per outing.

## 2. Cold email template (value-first, <80 words)

> **Subject:** A real-reward perk for [Gym Name] customers
>
> Hi [First],
>
> I noticed [Gym] runs [yoga class / student snacks / morning rush]. I run
> Emerge, a habit app used by [students at X / local fitness people].
>
> We can add **[Gym]** as a *sponsored quest*: your customers who finish a
> 2-week habit streak earn a real reward — e.g., a free shake or one class —
> redeemable at your counter.
>
> You get warm foot traffic and repeat visits, and we do the build and the
> tracking. We sign sponsors for $250/mo. [VALIDATE: confirm the naira fee +
> FX anchor before sending — quote in naira for Nigerian sponsors]
>
> Would you have 15 minutes this week to see a 2-minute example?
>
> — [Name]

Redemption mechanic to make explicit (never leave it vague): the customer shows
the in-app quest card / voucher code at the counter, the staff redeem the reward
(free shake / one class) and scan the code so foot traffic is counted. State
exactly that in the email and the one-pager.

**Nigeria rule:** email is the draft; **send it as a WhatsApp DM + voice note
instead** for Nigerian prospects (WhatsApp is the meeting-equivalent there, per
the Nigeria Playbook in SKILL.md).

### Follow-up sequence (each touch adds new value)

1. **Day 3:** add one local proof point (e.g., "a competitor two blocks away
   already ranked #2 in quests" or a waiting-list stat).
2. **Day 8:** send a one-line mock: "here's the exact storefront sign/QR we'd
   print."
3. **Day 15:** last-touch, close the file: "closing our January sponsor slots;
   reply YES and I'll send the one-pager."

Cadence evidence: first email ~58% of replies; follow-up 1 is the highest
performer (~8.4%); follow-up 2 adds ~3%; stop after the 4th (spam risk
triples). "Just checking in" reduces meetings booked by 14%. Subject lines
36–50 chars; one CTA; under ~80 words (Woodpecker,
woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/).

## 3. DM / WhatsApp template (native for local businesses)

2–3 sentences, one question, **no link in the first message**.

> Hi [First] — saw [Your Store] does [specific thing]. I run Emerge, a habit
> app popular with [students / fitness people] here. We can turn [Your Store]
> into a *sponsored quest* where your customers who finish a 2-week streak win
> a real reward from you (free [item/class]) — so you only "pay" when someone
> actually walks through your door. Worth a quick 5-minute chat this week?

Nigeria: follow up with a **voice note** — it's the WhatsApp-native equivalent
of a meeting and builds far more trust than text.

## 4. LinkedIn opener (after connect)

> Hi [First] — just connected. Saw [Gym] does [specific thing]. Small thought:
> we let local businesses sponsor a **habit quest** in the Emerge app (used by
> [segment]); customers who finish their streak get a real reward from you — so
> you only "pay" when someone actually walks through your door. Worth a 10-minute
> call this week? If not, happy to stay connected for when it fits.

Batch by common denominator ("gyms within 3 km of UNILAG", "cafés on the Yaba
axis") — tightly-defined campaigns outperform broad ones.

## 5. Creator partnership template

> Hey [Creator] — love your [study/fitness] content. I work with a habit app
> used by [students / 9–5s]. Want to run a *sponsored quest* for your audience?
> Your people who finish a 2-week habit streak get a real reward (e.g., discount
> at a partner store) — I'll give you a personal code that pays you per
> redemption, plus a feature subscription. Zero upfront cost to you. Interested
> in a 10-min call?

**Offer ladder (start cheap, pay on performance):**
1. Gifting + affiliate code (free premium + a promo code they earn on).
2. Flat micro-deal for 2–4 UGC TikTok/IG posts (`[VALIDATE]`: Nigerian
   micro-creator deals transact at a fraction of international anchors — test
   ₦50k–₦200k per campaign).
3. Revenue share on redemptions driven by their code.
4. A sponsored quest for their niche (fitness creator → "30-day workout" quest).

Best practices: several niche micro-creators over one big name; audit for fake
followers; request demographic data; license content for reuse (~$50 usage line)
so voucher campaigns get more mileage. Proof to quote: OLIPOP-style creator
codes grew creator-driven sales from ~3% → ~20% of mix (IMH).

## 6. Community-leader question script (10-min call)

1. "How do you currently keep your members active between events?"
2. "If you could give members a free tool that built a visible habit (e.g.,
   reading), would that help?"
3. "Would you be open to a 2-week challenge I run for free, promoted by you,
   with real rewards?" (Get a yes before any money talk.)
4. "If we formalize it, would a sponsored challenge from a local business that
   pays for member prizes make sense?" (Bridge toward a sponsor.)

Partnership shapes: campus clubs (sponsor a "30-day discipline/reading"
challenge; club promotes it; prize pool), faith youth groups (wellness/study
framing, keep the app secular), professional/alumni associations ("career edge"
framing), ambassador model (1–2 per campus; free premium + commission for
signups).

## 7. Pricing tiers (flat monthly sponsorship)

| Tier | Deliverables | Fee anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | 1 sponsored quest/mo, in-app reward via voucher platform, sponsor logo link, monthly redemption report | ~$150 (or launch-discounted) |
| Standard | 1–2 quests/mo rotated, sponsor QR at redemption, one in-app banner, monthly report + 1 co-marketing story | $250–350 |
| Partner | 2–3 quests, in-store signage kit, quarterly challenge co-hosted with a community/creator, full redemption analytics | $400–500+ |

- Per-campaign only as a 2-week "pilot quest" to de-risk Sponsor #1, then
  convert to monthly by month 2.
- Nigeria: anchor in naira (`[VALIDATE]` FX): roughly ₦300k–₦800k/mo at
  2025–26 rails; consider tiered naira pricing (₦150k starter single quest →
  ₦400k+ bundle); keep a discounted "launch partner" tier; require the first
  payment to clear before the quest goes live; monthly auto-renew with 30-day
  notice.
- Payment: monthly via bank transfer/USSD, invoiced via Paystack (never require
  a card). Paystack: local cards 1.5% + ₦100 (₦100 waived under ₦2,500; capped
  ₦2,000), transfers ₦10–₦50 (`[VALIDATE]` current — archived pricing).
- Pricing logic to put on the one-pager: a smoothie bar's average order value ×
  foot-traffic conversion makes one *incremental* reward-driven customer worth
  ~₦2,000–₦5,000; at $250/mo the sponsor only needs a handful of genuinely new
  customers/mo to break even (`[VALIDATE]` locally).

## 8. ROI scorecard (promise only what you can count)

1. Quest views / impressions (app reach — you control).
2. Voucher redemptions / foot traffic (unique codes scanned = walk-ins).
3. Repeat-visit rate (sponsor POS + your issue data).
4. Social / creator impressions (UTM-coded links).
5. Sponsor CAC = fee ÷ new customers — show it beats their paid-ad CAC.

Deliver an **end-of-month one-page scorecard** — it is the renewal and upsell
weapon.

## 9. One-page sponsor agreement (structure)

Plain language, both parties sign. Sections:
1. **Parties** — legal names + business addresses.
2. **Scope of sponsorship** — which quest, reward offered, redemption terms,
   how customers claim.
3. **Fee & payment** — monthly flat fee, currency, method (Paystack/transfer),
   invoice schedule, late-payment grace, refunds (none for completed months).
4. **Term & renewal** — start date, initial term, auto-renew monthly, 30-day
   written notice to cancel.
5. **Deliverables** — what Emerge builds and when (quest live by [date], voucher
   integration, monthly report); sponsor obligations (honor rewards, clear
   pricing staff).
6. **Usage / IP** — Emerge may use sponsor name/logo in-app + marketing;
   sponsor may share "Emerge partner" badge; content ownership of quest
   text/report.
7. **Termination** — either party 30 days; Emerge may pause live quests if fee
   unpaid; outstanding redemptions honored on termination.
8. **Limitation of liability** — Emerge not liable beyond fees paid; both
   independent contractors.
9. **Signatures** + date.

**Guarantee vs promise:** guarantee the deliverable (quest live, rewards
redeemable, monthly numbers reported); *promise* (as targets, not guarantees)
"customers will come" — the voucher sheet is the proof.

## 10. Lead pipeline (free CRM)

HubSpot Free CRM (2 users, 1,000 contacts, deal pipelines, email tracking,
meeting scheduler — hubspot.com/products/crm) or a simple Notion/Trello board
with columns: `Lead → Contacted → Pitched → Demo → Proposal → Signed → Live →
Renewal`. Per-lead card: business type, owner, channel, personalized hook, next
follow-up date, offer tier, status.

Lightweight scoring (0–10 on): (1) fit (storefront, foot traffic, complementary
niche), (2) warmth (replied vs cold), (3) speed (can meet this week). Chase 7–10s;
park the rest on a nurture list with UTM-tagged content.

## 11. 60-day checklist (fast-track to Sponsor #1)

- [ ] Day 1–10: 30-lead list (businesses in 3-block radius of target campus +
  5 campus clubs + 5 creators); HubSpot free; one-pager + sample quest + voucher
  mock + follow-up sequence.
- [ ] Day 11–25: 3 emails + 2 DMs per lead (4–7 touches, follow-ups every 3–4
  days); track replies.
- [ ] Day 26–35: 2 walk-in outings/week, off-peak, one concierge visit per store.
- [ ] Day 36–50: 2-week pilot quest at launch-discounted fee → convert to monthly.
- [ ] Day 51–60: publish redemption scorecard; sign sponsorship #2 on the back
  of #1's result.
